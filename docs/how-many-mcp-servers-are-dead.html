How many MCP servers are dead?

Direct answer from a read-only scan of the 9,991 remote endpoints our 2026-10-19 registry sweep returned · scanned 2026-10-19 · full report

Short answer: 57 endpoints failed outright (0.6%), and 5,183 (51.9%) gave no anonymous response — but the 51.9% is an upper bound on breakage, not a death count. 4,751 (47.6%) completed a clean anonymous handshake. Of those 5,183, 3,182 answered 401 and are classified AUTH_GATED, and 2,001 never answered at all. The no-response bucket still mixes genuinely dead servers with endpoints that require authentication by design, and the registry does not label the difference. An agent consuming the registry today cannot tell "requires OAuth" from "dead on arrival" without trying and failing.

Why the honest answer is a range, not a number

A weekly rescan adds the churn dimension: 115 endpoints that passed one week gave no response the next (2.4% of the passing set), so "dead" is also a moving target. From the November 2026 rescan onward, endpoints that answer 401 with a WWW-Authenticate: Bearer header and expose live RFC 9728 resource metadata are classified AUTH_GATED (verified) instead of unknown, which shrinks the gray bucket with every scan. Among servers that did respond, the dominant quality finding was 46,961 missing property descriptions — schema hygiene is a separate tax from uptime.
