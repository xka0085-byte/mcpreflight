How many MCP servers are dead?

Direct answer from a read-only scan of all 9,991 remote endpoints in the official MCP Registry · week of October 13, 2026 · full report

Short answer: 43 endpoints failed outright (0.4%), and 5,069 (50.7%) gave no anonymous response — but the 50.7% is an upper bound on breakage, not a death count. 4,879 (48.8%) completed a clean anonymous handshake. The no-response bucket mixes genuinely dead servers with endpoints that require authentication by design, and the registry does not label the difference. An agent consuming the registry today cannot tell "requires OAuth" from "dead on arrival" without trying and failing.

Why the honest answer is a range, not a number

A weekly rescan adds the churn dimension: 115 endpoints that passed one week gave no response the next (2.4% of the passing set), so "dead" is also a moving target. From the November 2026 rescan onward, endpoints that answer 401 with a WWW-Authenticate: Bearer header and expose live RFC 9728 resource metadata are classified AUTH_GATED (verified) instead of unknown, which shrinks the gray bucket with every scan. Among servers that did respond, the dominant quality finding was 46,216 missing property descriptions — schema hygiene is a separate tax from uptime.
