We run a read-only protocol-layer scanner over the endpoints registered in the official MCP Registry (9,991 servers, weekly rescans, ~50% currently complete a clean anonymous handshake). TDQS caught our attention because it scores the layer above where our checks stop — and the two stage structures barely overlap, which seems worth making explicit.

**Where our data touches the framework**

Most registry endpoints never reach Stage 3 (LLM rubric): they fail earlier, at the protocol layer your Stage 2 hard gates assume is working — no clean `initialize` + `tools/list` handshake, schemas missing `required`, empty `inputSchema` after Zod effect-wrapping, tools with undeclared required properties. From the client's perspective these are indistinguishable from "bad tool definitions," but no amount of prompt improvement scores them usefully because there is nothing valid to score.

**Two concrete suggestions**

1. The hard-gate stage could distinguish "definition is poor" (TDQS's domain) from "endpoint is unreachable/protocol-broken" (our domain). A registry-scale prefilter signal would keep LLM spend focused on definitions that can actually be evaluated — roughly half the registry, in our sample.
2. Correlation question worth checking: do servers with no anonymous handshake systematically score worse on your server-coherence dimension, or are they mostly auth-gated deployments that are perfectly fine? Our weekly data can't answer that alone; combined with TDQS scores it could.

Per-server reports with full methodology and a JSON API (weekly rescans, MIT tooling): https://xka0085-byte.github.io/mcp-doctor/trust/ — happy to share aggregate failure-shape distributions or cross-reference your per-tool scores if useful for the "TDQS across the registry" section.
